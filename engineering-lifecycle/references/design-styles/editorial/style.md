# Editorial

Magazine-inspired: expressive typography, strong imagery, asymmetric grids, and
carefully structured long-form content.

## The precondition

Editorial layout needs editorial content. A magazine grid applied to three feature
bullets and a stock photo produces something that looks like a magazine and reads
like nothing. If there is no real long-form copy and no real imagery, this is the
wrong style.

## Type

This is where the style lives.

- **Display:** high-contrast serif or a distinctive display face. `clamp(2.5rem, 6vw, 6rem)`,
  tracking `-0.02em`, leading `0.95` to `1.05`.
- **Body:** a serif optimised for reading (Tiempos Text, Source Serif, Charter) or
  a humanist sans. `1.125rem` to `1.25rem`, leading `1.7`.
- **Measure is the rule that matters: 60 to 75 characters.** Wider and the eye
  loses the line return. This constraint is non-negotiable regardless of how wide
  the layout is.
- **Standfirst / deck**: larger than body, lighter weight, sits between headline
  and body. It is a distinct level, not a big paragraph.
- Drop caps, small caps for bylines, and old-style figures in running text are all
  legitimate here and nowhere else.

## Grid

Asymmetric, and asymmetric for a reason.

- A 12-column grid where the text column occupies 6-7 and starts off-centre.
- Pull quotes and images break the measure deliberately, into the margin.
- Full-bleed images alternate with constrained text to create rhythm.
- The white space on the wide side is part of the composition, not a gap.

## Colour

- Paper-like background (`#FDFCFA`) or true dark for a night reading mode.
- Text near-black but not black.
- One accent, typically used only for links and the rule under a section heading.
- Imagery supplies the colour. The layout stays quiet.

## Motion

Almost none. Text that moves while you are reading it is hostile. Images may fade
in; type should not.

## Failure modes

- **Layout without content.** The most common failure by far.
- **Serif chosen because "creative"** rather than because this publication has a
  voice that suits it. See the anti-slop register, section 2.
- **Measure ignored** on wide screens, producing 140-character lines.
- **Pull quotes duplicating adjacent text**, so the reader reads it twice.
- **Rotated vertical text and section-number eyebrows**, which are agency-portfolio
  tells rather than editorial ones.
